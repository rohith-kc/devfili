# DevFili PRD (build version)

Status: locked for v1. Date: 2026-10-24. This file is the builder's copy of the product requirements; where it differs from earlier chat notes, this file wins.

## 1. What it is
A website where a developer picks an app (or asks for any other app) and gets a guide answering four questions: how hard is the OAuth app approval, how long (effort and wait as separate ranges), what must be prepared and what nuances apply, and what the agent does versus what the developer must do. Every claim carries an evidence label.

Why: provider OAuth approval is a launch blocker that is not code, with a huge spread: published developer reports show Zoom marketplace review of 7-10 business days plus fix time, Salesforce security-review queues of 6-9 weeks, Slack review clocks up to about 12 weeks, Google restricted-scope security assessments repeating every 12 months, and Microsoft Teams customer-admin consent per customer.

## 2. Goals, non-goals
Goals: (1) the four questions answered on every guide; (2) visible trust: every claim labeled Tested, Sourced or Uncertain; (3) works on apps we did not prepare via live research in the same format; (4) shareable link readable by a founder or PM.

Non-goals (v1): integration code; storing credentials; signing in to provider portals; creating or submitting provider apps; billing; teams/orgs; sign-in other than GitHub; the other 91 library apps; guaranteeing any timeline.

Success targets: verdict visible without scrolling on laptop and phone; 100% of claims labeled; 9 complete library guides; live guide in 3 minutes or less for at least 8 of 10 unfamiliar apps; 25 claims checked against real sources with results published; Zoom, Teams and HubSpot guides reviewed by the product owner against firsthand experience with corrections logged.

## 3. Users and jobs
Builder (engineer who owns the integration), Decider (founder/PM who commits dates), Customer admin (not a user; receives a note). Core job: when about to promise an integration, know the real path to an approved OAuth app so a date and scope can be committed to. Emotional job: do not make me look bad for a delay I could not predict.

## 5. Library apps and tile order (3 x 3)
Row 1: Zoom, Slack, Gong. Row 2: HubSpot, Notion, Linear. Row 3: Microsoft Teams, Google (Gmail), LinkedIn.
Why: covers every tier, a long-wait review, an easy baseline, a niche app, a scope fork, the customer-admin gate, partner-gated access. Product owner has firsthand experience with Zoom, Teams and HubSpot (the CRM Omnibound integrated), used as ground truth. Starting hypotheses (verify, never publish unverified): Zoom Tier 2; Slack Tier 2 with a long wait; Gong unknown; HubSpot likely Tier 1-2; Notion likely Tier 1-2; Linear likely Tier 1; Teams customer-admin flag applies; Google Tier 3 for restricted Gmail scopes; LinkedIn Tier 4 for marketing/community APIs.

## 7. Core flows
A. Browse: Home, pick tile, guide page, use helpers, share.
B. Ask: chat box, enter name or URL. If in library, open its guide. If not: resolve app, research live with streamed stages, show guide in the same format, optionally save (requires sign-in), share.
C. Save and share: library guides have links; live guides get one when a signed-in developer saves. Links are read-only and show last verified date.
Edge cases: ambiguous name (ask one question with options before researching); app uses API keys only (say so plainly, stop, no guide); docs unreachable (guide marked Incomplete, unverified claims Uncertain); ceiling reached (return what exists, marked Incomplete, or a clear timeout message if no guide could be drafted); app not found (clear message, suggest the website address).

## 8. Screens and UX
Home: banner, logo, one-line "what you can do", 9 tiles (name, tier badge, effort range, wait range, flags Gated / Customer admin, Hand-verified badge), chat button and Sign in top right (after sign-in shows name and link to My guides).
Guide page: laptop = main column plus sticky right rail "DevFili can help" (helpers); phone = rail becomes a section under the verdict. Verdict always at the very top.
Chat panel states: idle, asking a clarifying question, researching (list of stages, several may be active), done (open guide, save), could not find, error.
My guides: list newest first with app, saved date, share link, delete; empty state.
Shared guide: same as guide page, read-only, last verified date, no chat required.
UX rules: verdict first; ranges never single numbers and every range shows its basis; evidence labels always visible; owner tag at the start of every step; plain friendly tone; works at phone width; keyboard and screen reader support.
UI strings for research stages: 1 "Working out which app you mean", 2 "Finding the developer docs and review pages", 3 "Reading scopes, requirements and review steps", 4 "Looking for delays and rejections others hit", 5 "Checking that the pages and steps exist", 6 "Writing the guide in the standard format", 7 "Checking every claim against its source", 8 "Done: open the guide or save it".
Logo: simple SVG mark (two overlapping circles, suggesting friends) plus the wordmark "DevFili". Create it in code.

## 9. Guide content model (15 sections, same order every time)
1 Header (app, library/live badge, last verified date, completeness flag, owner name for saved guides, share) · 2 Verdict (tier, one-line reason, effort range, wait range, gate flags, confidence) · 3 Do you need your own app yet? · 4 How the integration works · 5 Scopes (scope, what it allows, needed for, review impact) · 6 Prepare before you start · 7 Steps (ordered, owner tag, time estimate) · 8 The three gates (provider, customer admin, end user: yes/no/unknown, who acts, what) · 9 Where it gets stuck (at least two, each sourced, with fix and escalation) · 10 While you wait · 11 Keeps coming back · 12 Hard to undo · 13 DevFili helpers for this app · 14 What we could not verify (always shown) · 15 Sources (always shown).
Completeness: Complete only if verdict tier is known, scopes >= 1, prerequisites >= 1, steps >= 3 with owner tags, all three gates present, stuck points >= 2, each backed by at least one non-Uncertain claim. Otherwise Incomplete with the thin sections listed.

## 10. Tier, effort, wait, evidence
Tiers: 1 Self-serve (register and use, no review before real users); 2 Reviewed (verification, marketplace or listing review before real users connect); 3 Security assessed (deep or third-party assessment, possibly recurring); 4 Partner-gated (access depends on business case, vetting or partnership, can be denied). Assignment (code, in order, first match wins): partner-gated, then security assessed, then review required, else Tier 1 only when all three are explicitly false; unknown facts give Unknown, never Tier 1 by default. Customer admin is a flag, not a tier. Scope forks are shown as "With only X, Tier a; adding Y moves it to Tier b".
Effort = developer hands-on time, waiting excluded (sum of "You do" step estimates). Wait = time from submission to decision for one review, each rejection adds a cycle. Ranges show basis: Official, Reported (community, with source age), Estimated (always Uncertain).
Confidence: High if tier facts and wait range come from official sources and are not Uncertain; Medium for a mix; Low if estimated or tier facts uncertain or only community sources older than two years.
Labels: Tested = our own server re-opened a public page and confirmed specific terms; Sourced = taken from a cited page (shown as re-checked when our server confirmed the quoted fragment, not re-checked when the page could not be read, for example JavaScript-only pages); Uncertain = inferred, conflicting, source unreachable or fragment missing, or estimated. Timelines are never Tested. When official and community sources disagree both are shown and the claim is Uncertain. Community sources older than two years carry an "old source" mark. Library guides older than 90 days show a re-verify notice. Library guides reviewed by a person show "Hand-verified" with the date.

## 11. Helpers
Owner tags: Agent did (research results already in the guide), Agent drafted (a draft to review), You do (needs the developer's login, identity, legal sign-off or a human conversation).
Must: (1) Tailored checklist: from guide steps, tick boxes, copy as text, no model call. (2) Scope justification draft: input product description and chosen scopes. (3) Reviewer test plan: input reviewer sign-up and main flow; uses placeholders for test accounts, never real credentials. (4) Customer admin note: input company and app name; only enabled when the customer-admin gate applies or is unknown. (5) Pre-submission check: from the guide's stuck points, tick list with remaining count, no model call.
Behaviour: helper inputs are used for the draft and never stored; helpers never ask for secrets and reject input that looks like a key or token; every draft is marked "Draft: review before use"; DevFili does not sign in, create or edit apps, pay fees, verify businesses, join review calls, submit, or run commands in a customer environment.

## 12. Test and verification plan
Product tests: T1 home on laptop and phone width shows banner, logo, 9 tiles, chat button, Sign in, no overflow. T2 each tile shows tier, effort, wait. T3 each library guide has all 15 sections, verdict visible without scrolling, every step tagged. T4 every claim labeled, every Sourced link opens, dates present, "could not verify" box shown. T5 unfamiliar app: stages stream in order, guide in 3 minutes or less, same format. T6 ambiguous name asks one question. T7 API-key-only app says so, no guide. T8 save a live guide and open its link in a private window: read-only with verified date. T9 each Must helper returns a draft marked as draft and a saved guide holds no helper input. T10 simulated unreachable docs gives an Incomplete guide that invents nothing. T11 a page containing instructions aimed at the agent is ignored. T12 keyboard-only use and labels distinguishable without colour. T13 sign in with GitHub, save, sign out, sign in again: guide is under the developer's name, a guest cannot save. T14 delete shows confirmation, guide leaves My guides, its link shows not-found.
Accuracy check: 25 claims, 5 each from Zoom, Teams, Slack, Google and one live-generated app; each app's 5 cover a timeline, a requirement, a scope impact, a gate and a rejection reason; each result is Correct, Corrected (what changed) or Unverified (why); totals and most common error type go in the README. Ground-truth review: the product owner reviews Zoom, Teams and HubSpot guides section by section (matches, missing, wrong, out of date); anything missed that they know from experience becomes a new check in the research step. Unfamiliar-app run before submission on 10 apps: Calendly, Salesforce, Asana, Dropbox, Atlassian Jira, Intercom, Mailchimp, Shopify, Box, Typeform; target complete guides for at least 8, honest failure messages for the rest.

## 13. Risks and honest limitations
Risks and mitigations: wrong timeline stated confidently (ranges with basis, Uncertain labels, hand verification); old sources (age marks, last verified date, re-verify notice after 90 days); unreadable pages (Incomplete flag); live generation slow or failing in a demo (library pre-generated, recent results reused, time ceiling, partial results, rehearsal); prompt injection (web text is data; no tools that act on accounts); cost abuse (per-visitor daily limit and result reuse); vendor-written sources lean toward selling a fix (source type shown, official ranked first); scope creep (Must/Should priorities); shared link stops working (deleting warns first); free database pauses when unused (library is static in the repo; keepalive; check before submitting); sign-in adds build time and personal data (store only GitHub ID, name, avatar; no tokens); free model limits or a rejected key (pre-generate library, reuse results, about 3 Gemini calls per guide, try-again state, provider fallback, check-keys before building).
State openly in the README: DevFili never creates real provider apps so it has never seen a real approval and review times are reports, not measurements; "Tested" means a public page or documented step was checked, never that a flow was completed; only nine apps have reviewed guides and every other app is researched live and not human-reviewed; this is research, not legal or compliance advice; provider rules change so each guide shows its last verified date.
